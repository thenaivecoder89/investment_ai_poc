Blended Finance
Project Description
The proposed financing is a green corporate loan facility of up to €250 million for the benefit of Elementum Energy Limited (“Elementum” or the “Company”), provided through its wholly owned subsidiaries Dnistrovska Vitroelektrostantsiia LLC and Windpark West-R LLC as co-borrowers. The facility will support the development and construction of two greenfield wind power projects in Ukraine: the 57.6 MW Danube project and the 200 MW Ternopil project (also referred to as “Windpark West-R”).
In addition, viability gap funding may be provided by IFC as implementing entity of the Clean Technology Fund for a pilot 10 MW/20 MWh battery energy storage system (“BESS”) which is located at one of the Company’s existing facilities.
The project is expected to be supported by partners of (i) the European Commission under the Ukraine Investment Framework (“EC-UIF”), (ii) the Economic Resilience Action Program (“ERA Program”) for Ukraine including support from the United Kingdom, acting through the Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office, and (iii) the Clean Technology Fund (“CTF”), as described in the Blended Finance Section.
Associated Advisory Engagement
IFC provided upstream support to the project in the form of legal and market analyses, which informed the structuring process and enhanced the project’s overall bankability.
Sponsor / Cost / Location
Project Sponsor and Major Shareholders of Project Company
Elementum is a leading independent power producer and the second largest renewable energy operator in Ukraine. Elementum owns a diversified portfolio of 28 solar power plants (536 MW) and one wind power plant (100 MW) across seven regions. Elementum was established in 2019 and is wholly owned by VR Global Partners, L.P., an international asset manager specializing in distressed and special situations in emerging markets, with a longstanding presence in Ukraine since 2014.
Total Project Cost and Amount and Nature of IFC's Investment
The total project cost is estimated to be approximately €400 million. IFC is considering providing an A Loan of up to €60 million for IFC’s own account and mobilize financing through a B Loan and Parallel Loans from other lenders.
Blended Finance
IFC as implementing entity of the ERA Program for Ukraine, and CTF alongside EC-UIF, is expected to provide co-investments of €79 million, including first-loss guarantees for IFC and other lenders, a senior loan, and viability gap funding to help mitigate high country and project risks constraining IFC’s and other lenders’ participation in the project. The level of concessionality (i.e., “subsidy”) provided by the instruments is estimated to be 9% of the total project cost of approximately €400 million. This estimate is based on the difference between (i) a “reference price” (either a market price if available; the price calculated using IFC’s pricing model, which comprises three main elements: risk, cost and profit; or a negotiated price) and (ii) the “concessional price” being charged by the instruments.

As part of IFC’s commitment to transparency in the deployment of concessional blended finance resources, similar instruments have been made available to other projects of a similar nature in Ukraine. Further details and historical information on estimated subsidy levels in IFC’s blended finance portfolio can be found at: https://www.ifc.org/en/what-we-do/sector-expertise/blended-finance. As is the case with all of IFC’s blended concessional finance co-investments, this project has been assessed against the Enhanced Blended Concessional Finance Principles for DFI Private Sector Operations adopted by IFC and more than 20 other DFIs in 2017. Further information on these Enhanced Principles and IFC’s blended finance approach and governance can also be found at: https://www.ifc.org/en/what-we-do/sector-expertise/blended-finance.
Location of Project and Description of Site
The Danube project and the BESS pilot project are located in southern Ukraine; the Ternopil project is located in western Ukraine.
Development Impact
Anticipated Impact Measurement & Monitoring (AIMM) Assessment
The project will add clean and affordable power generation capacity, which is required to avoid imminent supply shortage in a fragile and conflict-affected market undergoing extreme duress. The project will demonstrate the viability of the wholesale market by supporting two greenfield power projects operating on a fully merchant basis, with strong replication potential for other market participants. It will contribute to the diversification of Ukraine’s energy mix and to strengthening the country’s energy security.
IFC's Role and Additionality
IFC will provide a long-term innovative financing structure tailored to support the overall viability of the project in a context of exceptionally high uncertainty, with de-risking through blended finance. IFC is providing support to the Company on preparing a green finance framework and on the management of potential environmental and social (E&S) risks in line with IFC Performance Standards (PS).

E&S Category Rationale / Risks and Mitigation
Environmental & Social Categorization Rationale
This is a Category B project according to IFC's Policy on Environment and Social Sustainability (2012) with potential limited adverse environmental or social risks and/or impacts that are few in number, generally site-specific, largely reversible, and readily addressed through mitigation measures.
E&S risks and impacts associated with the investment relate to: (i) adequacy of ESIAs in line with IFC PS; (ii) company’s and contractor’s E&S governance, oversight and management capacity and systems; (iii) contractor E&S management; (iv) labor, working conditions and OHS, including in contractor workforce and the supply chain, and worker grievance mechanism; (v) water and waste management; (vi) community health, safety and security including impacts from shadow flicker, blade throw, noise, and traffic; (vii) land acquisition and land based livelihoods; (viii) biodiversity impacts, particularly risks posed to birds and bats; and (ix) stakeholder engagement and external grievance mechanism.
