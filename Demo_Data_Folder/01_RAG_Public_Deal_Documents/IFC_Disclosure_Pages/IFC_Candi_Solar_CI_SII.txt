Project Description
Candi Solar (Candi or the “Company”) is a dedicated solar installer, financier and operator in India and South Africa, headquartered in Switzerland. Candi started operations from India in January 2018 and began South Africa operations 18 months later. Candi offers its Commercial & Industrial (C&I) customers a zero upfront and maintenance cost rooftop solar photovoltaic (PV) solution with a wide range of products to provide solar solutions as per needs of the customers. The various products/models include power purchase agreements,  instalment sales, and loans, with terms ranging from 3 years to over 20 years and systems from 100KWp to over 5 MWp. As of November 2024, its project portfolio stood at 118.2MWp, of which 76MWp are operational, 35.6MWp are in construction and  6.0MWp have been contracted. The current split of the Company’s portfolio between India and South Africa is c. 80:20 with a target split of 50:50.
Candi is seeking to raise funding to meet its two-year capital expenditure (CAPEX) plan to increase its solar PV and battery energy storage solutions (BESS) portfolio by c.150MWp in India and South Africa (the Project).
For the proposed investment, an IFC borrower entity will be created in India and South Africa i.e. Candi Solar Asset Hld PTY Limited (“IFC SA AssetCo” or “SA Borrower”) and Candi Solar IN 3 PVT Limited (“IFC IN AssetCo” or “IN Borrower”), together referred as the Borrowers.” IFC proceeds will be used in developing new asset portfolios based on the Candi’s pipeline. Most potential projects in the immediate pipeline are less than 5 MW except for four projects in India with capacities between 5 and 15 MW.
Sponsor / Cost / Location
Project Sponsor and Major Shareholders of Project Company
Candi solar AG, incorporated under the laws of Switzerland in January 2018 is a platform that finances, builds and operates solar systems for SMEs in emerging markets (India and South Africa).
The shareholders include STOA (22.9%), Norfund(18.5%), FPCI Energy Access Ventures (8.8%), Energy Entrepreneurs Growth Fund(5.2%), Persistent Energy Capital (3.7%), Gaia Energy Impact Fund(3.6%), Scobag Privatbank AG (3.6%), Kyuden International Europe BV(3.2%), Angels(14 individuals) (3.5%), and the Founders + Employees(27%).
Total Project Cost and Amount and Nature of IFC's Investment
The Project Cost is US$100m with proposed investment being a secured debt financing package of US$65m - consisting of an (a) IFC A Loan of US$40m; (b) a US$10m subordinated loan from the Clean Technology Fund; and (c) US$5m subordinated Loan from a Canadian fund; and (d) a MCPP Loan of US$10m. The remaining US$35m will be provided through shareholders’ equity, to fund the CAPEX plan.
Blended Finance
The proposed climate funds structured as subordinated loans comprise of up to US$10m from the Clean Technology Fund and US$5m from a Canadian Fund (together “BF loans”).  The BF loans will be coinvested with IFC’s up to US$40m A loan and B-lender’s up to US$10 million senior loan to fund Candi’s two-year CAPEX to expand its portfolio by ~150MWp. The subordinated BF loan is key to addressing operational risk and improving DSCR of senior lenders, to an acceptable level to participate in the debt financing package given the high risk of this nascent sector and business model.
The full project costs are expected to be US$100m (equivalent of ZAR/INR/USD) which will be financed by IFC debt package of US$65m and sponsor equity of US$35m.

Location of Project and Description of Site
The Company is headquartered in Switzerland with operations in India and South Africa, serving SMEs that need reliable and affordable energy.
Development Impact
Anticipated Impact Measurement & Monitoring (AIMM) Assessment
IFC anticipates that the Project will have positive environmental effects by supporting the addition of rooftop solar, thereby helping reduce the GHG footprint of the electricity sector in South Africa and India. IFC also expects the Project to help meet energy demand by providing renewable energy at more competitive tariffs and result in savings for C&I customers. On the market side, IFC expects the Project to contribute to market competitiveness by supporting a private player in the growth stage of the C&I segment and help demonstrate the viability of rooftop solar among SMEs, with potential replicability. IFC also expects the project to contribute to the resilience of the power sector by supporting the diversification of power generation away from fossil fuel-based sources within the C&I market in South Africa.
IFC's Role and Additionality
IFC's additionality is both financial and non-financial. Financial additionality is provided through resource mobilization. Non-financial additionality derives from the application of IFC’s knowledge and experience significant know-how within the energy sector in South Africa and India. IFC’s participation also supports the application of its Performance Standards to mitigate E&S risks, in particular risks related to the construction and operation of the solar projects.

E&S Category Rationale / Risks and Mitigation
Environmental & Social Categorization Rationale
This is a Category B project according to IFC’s Policy on Environmental and Social Sustainability, as it is considered to involve potentially limited adverse environmental or social risks and impacts that are few in number, generally site-specific, largely reversible, and readily addressed through mitigation measures.
Key E&S risks associated with the Project will include: (a) company's E&S capacity and management systems to manage its operations (both during installation and operation) in line with IFC PSs; (b) contractor management (especially on health and safety) in line with IFC PSs requirements during installation and operation; (c) implementation of fair, safe and healthy working conditions in line with PS2 and legal requirements; (d) supply chain management for solar panels and batteries; (e) management of waste (broken panels, used batteries etc.) in line with WBG EHS Guidelines; and (f) community grievance mechanism.
